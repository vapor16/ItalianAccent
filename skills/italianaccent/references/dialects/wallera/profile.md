# Wallera — Napoletano (city of Naples)

> Mode id `wallera` (commercial spelling of the attested word **uallera / guallera**, "hernia"; figuratively "boredom, annoyance", as in *me staje abbuffanno 'a uallera* "you're boring me to death"). In the dialect text always write **uallera** or **guallera**, never "wallera".

## 1. Identity & scope

- **Variety:** Neapolitan (*napulitano*) of the city of Naples, Campania. Upper-southern (intermediate-southern) Italo-Romance; ISO 639-3 `nap`. In Italy commonly called a dialect; linguistically the central variety of the Neapolitan group. [src-nap-enwiki-language, src-nap-fortunato-2017]
- **Norm adopted:** urban Neapolitan of 20th-century theatre, song and literature, written in the conventional modern spelling (articles `'o 'a 'e`, infinitives `magnà / vedé / sentì`). Not Irpinian, Cilentan, Beneventan, Casertan or the islands (which use `'u`). [src-nap-fortunato-2017]
- **Typological profile (what makes it Neapolitan, not "Italian with an accent"):** metaphony marks gender/number; neuter vs masculine distinguished by doubling after `'o`; `tené` = to have (possess), `avé` = auxiliary only; `avé 'a` = must; `stà` for location/temporary state; enclitic possessives on kinship nouns; prepositional accusative `a` with persons; `ca` complementizer. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica, src-nap-fortunato-2017]

## 2. Orthography

Adopted convention (see `metadata.orthographic_conventions`):
- **Apostrophes** mark lost sounds: articles/pronouns `'o 'a 'e` (from *lo la le*); aphaeresis `'ncoppa` (on top), `'mmiezo` (in the middle), `'nzerrà` (to close), `'nterra`, `'mmocca`, `'mmano`; apocope `so'` (I am / they are), `pe'` (for), `cu'` (with), `vo'` (wants), `po'` (then). [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica]
- **Final vowels** are written with their etymological letter (`-o -a -e`) although all unstressed finals are pronounced as schwa. Do not write `ə` or `ë` in running text. [src-nap-enwiki-language, src-nap-itwiki-lingua]
- **Accents:** obligatory on final stressed syllables (`magnà`, `pecché`, `accussì`, `Totò`); grave = open, acute = closed; otherwise optional, used only to show stress (`fràtemo`, `sòreta`). **Circumflex** marks crasis or a long contracted vowel: `ô` (a + 'o), `â` (a + 'a), `ê` (a + 'e), `dô`, `sî` (you are), `hê` (you have, aux). [src-nap-enwiki-language, src-nap-itwiki-grammatica]
- **j** for the glide: `jammo` (let's go), `aje`, `maje` (never), `juorno`, `jì` (to go). After a vowel `jì` often appears as `ghi'` (*pe' ghi' 'e pressa*). [src-nap-wikiquote-proverbi]
- **Syntactic doubling** is pronounced after many triggers (§4); in writing this profile shows it in the frequent, meaning-bearing cases: neuter `'o ppane`, feminine plural `'e ffemmene / 'e ccose`, pronouns `a mme`, `pe' tte`, and `cchiù` (more). Elsewhere it is optional. [src-nap-enwiki-language]
- Attested alternatives you will meet: `chiù` for `cchiù`; `nu/'nu`, `na/'na`; 19th-century `lo, la, li` and `-are` infinitives (archaic, avoid). [src-nap-dambra-1873, src-nap-wikiquote-proverbi]

## 3. Phonology visible in writing

- **Metaphony:** a final *-i/-u* raised the stressed vowel: e→i, o→u, è→ie, ò→uo. So masculine/plural forms differ in the stem: `niro / nera` (black m/f), `sicco / secca`, `russo / rossa`, `buono / bona`, `gruosso / grossa`, `viecchio / vecchia`, `nuovo / nova`; plurals `pesce → pisce`, `guaglione → guagliune`. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica, src-nap-fortunato-2017]
- **pl- → chi-**: `chiù/cchiù` (più), `chiano` (piano), `chiovere` (piovere), `chiena` (piena); **cl- → chi-** idem. **bi-/bl- → j-**: `janco` (bianco). **fi-/fl- → sci-**: `sciore` (fiore), `sciummo` (fiume). [src-nap-volpe-1869]
- **-nd- → -nn-**, **-mb- → -mm-**: `quanno`, `munno`, `mannà`, `scennere`, `annascunnere`. [src-nap-volpe-1869, src-nap-fortunato-2017]
- **b → v** (betacism) initially/intervocalically: `vocca` (bocca), `vasà` (baciare), `varca`, `vraccio`, `votta` (botte); doubled back to `bb` after triggers (`tre bbote`). [src-nap-volpe-1869, src-nap-fortunato-2017]
- **d → r** in speech (`Maronna`, `vere` for *vede*, `ricere`); writing keeps `d` in most words; use `r` only in fixed items (`Maronna`, `Marò`) and to sound very colloquial. [src-nap-volpe-1869, src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- **l + consonant → u/r**: `caudo/càvero` (caldo), `auto` (alto), `vota` (volta). [src-nap-volpe-1869]
- **Glide insertion** between vowels: `pajese` (paese), `voje` (buoi). [src-nap-volpe-1869]
- **s + consonant** is pronounced [ʃ] (`scuorno`, `spità`) but written `s`. [src-nap-enwiki-language]
- `-ll-` stays `-ll-` in Naples (`chillo`, `bello`, `cavallo`) — no `-dd-`. [src-nap-fortunato-2017]

## 4. Morphology

**Definite articles** [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica, src-nap-enwiki-language]

| | before consonant | before vowel |
|---|---|---|
| m. sg. | `'o` (`'o sole`) | `l'` (`l'amico`) |
| neuter sg. (mass/abstract nouns, languages, substantivized adjectives) | `'o` + doubling (`'o ppane`, `'o mmale`, `'o nnapulitano` = the Neapolitan language) | `l'` |
| f. sg. | `'a` (`'a casa`) | `l'` |
| m. pl. | `'e` (`'e guagliune`) | `ll'` (`ll'uommene`) |
| f. pl. | `'e` + doubling (`'e ffemmene`, `'e gguaglione` = the girls) | `ll'` |

Indefinite: `nu` (m./neut.), `na` (f.), `n'` before vowel (`n'amico`). No plural indefinite; use `cierti/certe` or nothing. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica]

**Articulated prepositions:** `d''o, d''a, d''e` (of the); `ô, â, ê` (to the); `c''o` (with the); `p''o` (for the); `dint'ô / 'int'ô` (in the); `'ncopp'ô` (on the); `addu` (at someone's place: `addu Ciro`). `'n-` prefix = in: `'ncielo`, `'nterra`, `'mmano`. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica]

**Nouns/adjectives:** gender and number are carried by the article, doubling and metaphony, not by the (schwa) final vowel: `'o guaglione / 'e guagliune` (boy/boys), `'a guagliona / 'e gguaglione` (girl/girls). Diminutives `-iello / -ella`, `-illo` (`piccerillo`, `vicariello`, `Ciccillo`); collective/abstract `-imma` (`calimma` heat, `cazzimma`). [src-nap-enwiki-language, src-nap-fortunato-2017]

**Subject pronouns:** `i'/io`, `tu`, `isso` (he), `essa` (she), `nuje`, `vuje` (also polite "you"), `lloro`. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica]
**Clitics:** `me, te, 'o` (him/it; neuter `'o` + doubling: `'o ttiene?`), `'a, ce` (us; also there), `ve, 'e` (them, + doubling), `se`; dative 3rd `le` / `ce`; partitive `ne`. Prepositional with doubling: `a mme`, `pe' tte`, `cu vvuje`. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica, src-nap-enwiki-language]
**Demonstratives:** `chisto/chesta/chesto` (this; neuter `chesto`), short `'stu/'sta`; `chillo/chella/chello` (that; neuter `chello` = that thing). Emphatic article + demonstrative: `chillo 'o professore`. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica, src-nap-fortunato-2017]
**Possessives** follow the noun: `'o sole mio`, `'a casa toja`, `'e figlie nuoste`. Forms: `mio/mia/mieje/meje`, `tujo/toja`, `sujo/soja`, `nuosto/nosta`, `vuosto/vosta`, `lloro`. **Enclitic** with kinship nouns (1st/2nd sg possessor): `màmmema, mammeta` (my/your mother), `pàtemo, pàteto`, `fràtemo, fràteto`, `sòrema, sòreta`, `muglièrema`. "A friend of mine" = `n'amico mio` / `n'amico d''o mio` (partitive). [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica, src-nap-fortunato-2017]

## 5. Verbs

Present indicative of the core verbs [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica; fà/dicere forms from attested proverbs, src-nap-wikiquote-proverbi]:

| | essere | avé (aux) | tené (have) | stà | jì (go) | fà | sapé | puté | vulé |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| i' | songo / so' | aggio | tengo | stongo | vaco | faccio | saccio | pozzo | voglio |
| tu | sî | haje / hê | tiene | staje | vaje | faje | saje | può | vuò |
| isso | è | ha / ave | tene | sta | va | fa | sape | pò | vo' |
| nuje | simmo | avimmo / âmmo | tenimmo | stammo | jammo | facimmo | sapimmo | putimmo | vulimmo |
| vuje | site | avite / âte | tenite | state | jate | facite | sapite | putite | vulite |
| lloro | songo / so' | hanno | teneno | stanno | vanno | fanno | sanno | ponno | vonno |

- **Metaphony in the present:** 2nd sg raises the stem: `veco / vide` (I see / you see), `sento / siente`, `corro / curre`, `tengo / tiene`, `moro / muore`. Infinitive `vedé/veré`, `sentì`, `murì`. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica]
- **tené vs avé:** possession and states use `tené` (`tengo famme`, `tengo ragione`, `nun tengo tiempo`). `avé` is only the auxiliary and in `avé 'a`. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica, src-nap-fortunato-2017]
- **Auxiliary:** `avé` for practically all verbs, including motion, *essere* and reflexives: `aggio juto` (sono andato), `aggio stato a Napule ajere`, `aggio venuto`; `essere` is possible with some intransitives (`so' caruto`). Participles: `fatto, ditto, juto, venuto, stato, visto, miso` (put), `pigliato`, `scritto`. [src-nap-enwiki-language, src-nap-itwiki-grammatica]
- **Past tenses:** passato prossimo in conversation (`aggio fatto`, `hê ditto`, `ha dditto` — doubling distinguishes 3sg from 2sg). Passato remoto is alive in narration: `facette` (fece), `dicette` (disse), `jette` (andò), `fuje` (fu), `parlaje` (parlò), `vedette`, 3pl `facettero / dicettero`; it is the tense of wellerisms (*dicette Pulecenella...*). Imperfect: `parlavo, parlave, parlava`; `jeva` (andava), `steva`, `faceva`, `era/ire`. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica, src-nap-wikiquote-proverbi]
- **Future:** the synthetic future is obsolete. Use the present + time adverb (`dimane vaco a Napule`) or `avé 'a` + infinitive (nuance of intention/necessity). [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica]
- **Obligation `avé 'a` (= dovere):** `aggi' 'a fà` (I must do), `hê 'a fà`, `ha dda fà` / `adda fà`, `âmm' 'a / avimm' 'a fà`, `âte 'a fà`, `hann' 'a fà`. Impersonal `s'adda fà` (it must be done). With clitic: `hâ fà` (you must do it). [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica, src-nap-wikiquote-proverbi]
- **Conditional:** largely replaced by the imperfect subjunctive: `vulesse` (I'd like), `facesse`, `putesse`; old `vurria` survives in songs (*i' te vurria vasà*). Hypotheticals often have subjunctive in both clauses: `si tenesse 'e sorde, m'accattasse 'a casa`; colloquially also imperfect indicative in both. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica, src-nap-fortunato-2017]
- **Progressive:** `stà` + gerund: `sto' parlanno`, `che staje facenno?`, `sta chiuvenno`. Gerunds `-anno / -enno`: `parlanno, facenno, dicenno, jenno`. [src-nap-enwiki-language, src-nap-itwiki-grammatica]
- **Imperative:** `fa'!`, `dimme!`, `jamme!` (let's go), `viene ccà!`, `sient'a mme!`, `statte zitto!`, `pigliate 'o caffè`. Negative imperative = `nun` + infinitive: `nun parlà`, `nun te piglià collera`. Repeated imperative for emphasis: `jamme ja`. [src-nap-fortunato-2017, src-nap-wikiquote-proverbi]
- Infinitives are apocopated and stressed: `-à` (parlà), `-é` (vedé, tené), `-ì` (sentì, venì), unstressed `-ere` (chiagnere, scennere, mettere). [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica]

## 6. Syntax

- **Negation:** `nun` (before vowels/pauses), `nu` before consonant with doubling (`nu ddicere niente`). Negative concord: `nun aggio visto a nisciuno`, `nun saccio niente`, `nun è maje stato`, `manco` (not even) `nun 'o saccio manco i'`. [src-nap-enwiki-language]
- **Prepositional accusative:** animate/definite human objects take `a`: `aggio visto a Pascale`, `chiamma a mammeta`, but `aggio visto 'a machina`. [src-nap-itwiki-grammatica, src-nap-fortunato-2017]
- **Complementizers:** `ca` = declarative "that" (`aggio ditto ca nun vengo`) and relative (`'o guaglione ca aggio visto`); `che` = interrogative/exclamative and triggers doubling (`che ppiense?`, `che ffemmena!`). [src-nap-enwiki-language, src-nap-fortunato-2017]
- **Interrogatives:** `che?` / `che è?` (what), `chi?`, `addò?` (where), `quanno?`, `comme?`, `pecché?`, `quanto?`; `'e do sî?` (where are you from?). Yes/no questions by intonation; tag `neh?` / `overo?` (really?). [src-nap-andreoli-1887, src-nap-enwiki-language]
- **Fronting & dislocation** are the norm in emotional speech: `'o ccafè, t''o piglio i'` ; `chesto nun t''o pozzo dicere`. Clitic doubling of objects is frequent.
- **Locative/existential:** `ce sta` / `nce sta` (there is), `ce stanno` (there are), `ccà`, `llà`, `lloco` (there, near you), `addò staje?`
- **Vocatives** are truncated after the stressed syllable: `guagliò!` (boy), `Gennà`, `dottò`, `signò`, `mammà`; with `uè` / `oi`: `uè, guagliò!`. [src-nap-enwiki-language]
- **Comparatives:** `cchiù... 'e` / `cchiù... ca`: `cchiù fetente 'e na recchia 'e cunfessore`. [src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- Infinitives after modals are normal (`pozzo venì`, `vulesse sapé`); `pe'` + infinitive for purpose (`pe' ghi' 'e pressa`).

## 8. Discourse markers & interjections

- `Uè!` / `Uhè!` — calling, greeting, surprise, protest (*Uè, guagliò!*). [src-nap-wikiquote-proverbi]
- `Neh?` — interrogative tag / "eh?" (*Viene stasera, neh?*). [src-nap-andreoli-1887]
- `Uh!` — surprise, dismay; `Uh Maronna!`, `Uh Mamma d''a Saletta!`. [src-nap-andreoli-1887, src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- `Marò!` / `Maronna mia!` — shock, exasperation (mild, not blasphemous). [src-nap-andreoli-1887]
- `Azz!` — wow / damn (surprise). [src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- `Jamme!`, `Jamme bell', ja!` — come on / hurry / "come off it". [src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- `Overo?` — really? [src-nap-andreoli-1887]
- `Mannaggia!` (+ `'a miseria`, `bubbà`, `'o suricillo e 'a pezza 'nfosa`) — frustration, from mild to strong. [src-nap-andreoli-1887, src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- `Mmalora!` — damn it (old). `Vattenne!` — go away / come on, no way. `Sciò!` — shoo. [src-nap-andreoli-1887]
- `Chiste so' nummere!` — "this is unbelievable" (play the numbers in the lotto). [src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- `E bravo 'o fesso!` — sarcastic applause. `Statte buono` — bye/take care. `Crisce santo!` — bless you. [src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- Fillers: `mo`, `e mo?`, `vabbuò` (ok, fine), `dico i'`, `sient'a mme`, `'o vvi'?` (see?).

## 9. Profanity palette by vulgarity level

Friendly vs hostile matters more than the word: the same `strunzo` is a laugh between friends and a declaration of war with a stranger.
- **0** — no vulgarity. Use `mannaggia`, `Marò`, `uh`, `fesso`-free irony; `me staje facenno ascì pazzo`.
- **1** — mild: `fesso` (fool), `cafone`, `scemo`, `chiachiello`, `mannaggia bubbà`, `'nzallanuto`.
- **2** — colloquial rude: `mappina` (rag, wreck), `fetente`, `curnuto e mazziato` (idiom), `chiaveca` (scoundrel), `pernacchio`.
- **3** — vulgar but common: `me staje abbuffanno 'a uallera`, `m'hê fatto 'a uallera a pezzaiola`, `'e merda`, `strunzià`, `cacasotto`, `cuglione`, `mannaggia l'anema toja`.
- **4** — strong: `strunzo`, `cazzo` (and `a faccia d''o cazzo!`, `coppole 'e cazzo`, `scassacazzo`), `va' fa' 'nculo`, `'o frate d''o cazzo`, `scarda 'e cesso`, `puortame a soreta`.
- **5** — very strong / taboo: `sfaccimma` (*che sfaccimma vaje truvanno?*), `ommo 'e sfaccimma`, `figlio 'e zoccola`, `pucchiacca`, curses on the dead (`mannaggia 'e muorte tuoje`, `chi t'è muorto`) — the gravest Neapolitan insult; **blasphemies** (`Mannaggia 'a Maronna`, unverified) only at 5 and never as decoration.
- Never generate slurs (`ricchione` etc., `generation_allowed: false`). Friendly vulgarity targets the situation (the bug, the day), hostile vulgarity is only for clearly requested venting about a third party and never for the user.

## 11. Humor, irony, sarcasm (devices)

- **Wellerisms** (*dicette 'o pappavallo...*, *dicette Pulecenella...*): put a maxim in the mouth of an animal or a mask. [src-nap-wikiquote-proverbi]
- **Grotesque hyperbole**, often culinary: *m'hê fatto 'a uallera a pezzaiola / a matriciana* ("you've made my hernia pizzaiola-style"). [src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- **Mock applause / antiphrasis:** *E bravo 'o fesso!*, *Steveme scarze a chiaveche!* ("we were short of scoundrels" = welcome, you were the missing one). [src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- **Rhetorical questions:** *Ma che m'ammacche?* (what fairy tale are you telling me?), *Tu ch'accucchie?* (what are you piecing together?). [src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- **Smorfia/lotto references:** *Chiste so' nummere!* — an absurd event is worth playing in the lotto. [src-nap-wikiquote-modididire]
- **Theatrical fatalism** (*Lassa correre 'o munno comme va*) and **understatement** of disasters (*È na varca scassata* — it's a wrecked boat).
- Humor is about situations and language, never about people from Naples (no "pizza, mandolino, camorra" stereotypes).

## 13. Transformation guide (AI-illustrative, based on documented rules)

1. *Devo andare a lavorare, ho fretta.* → **Aggi' 'a jì a faticà, vaco 'e pressa.** (avé 'a, jì, faticà, ghi' 'e pressa)
2. *Ieri sono andato da mio fratello.* → **Ajere aggio juto addu fràtemo.** (avé auxiliary with motion, addu, enclitic possessive)
3. *Hai visto Maria? Non ho visto nessuno.* → **Hê visto a Maria? Nun aggio visto a nisciuno.** (prepositional accusative, negative concord)
4. *Ho fame e non ho soldi.* → **Tengo famme e nun tengo sorde.** (tené for possession/states)
5. *Le donne dicono che il pane è buono.* → **'E ffemmene diceno ca 'o ppane è bbuono.** (f. pl. and neuter doubling, ca)
6. *Se avessi tempo, lo farei.* → **Si tenesse tiempo, 'o facesse.** (subjunctive in both clauses)
7. *Cosa stai facendo? Sto scrivendo il codice.* → **Che staje facenno? Sto' scrivenno 'o codice.** (stà + gerund)
8. *(tech) Prima di fare il deploy devi lanciare i test, altrimenti si rompe tutto.* → **Primma 'e fà 'o deploy hê 'a lancià 'e test, si no se scassa tutte cose.**

## 14. Contamination guard (must NOT appear)

- **Sicilian/Calabrian:** articles `u, a, i` without apostrophe (`u cani`), `-ddu` for `-llu` (`cavaddu`, `iddu`), complementizers `mi/mu/ma`, `chi` + finite verb for "to", `picciottu`, `carusu`, `minchia`, `mbare`, `talìa`, past `fici/dissi`. Naples: `'o`, `isso`, `chillo`, `ca`, infinitive.
- **Apulian/Salentine:** `ieu`, `quiddu`, `cu` + finite verb, `jind`, `u uagnone` (Bari), `mena`, `trimone`. Neapolitan: `guaglione`, `dinto`.
- **Roman:** `daje`, `er/li` articles, `mo' vabbè` Roman-style, `annamo`, `fregnaccia`. Neapolitan: `jamme`, `jammo`, `vabbuò`.
- **Other Campanian varieties:** `'u` / `i` articles (islands, Cilento, Irpinia: `iddu`, `u cane / i chene`), `-dd-` from `-ll-` (Irpinia/Cilento), forms marked "not used in Naples" (`sevo/seva`, Nolan `mecco/pergo`). [src-nap-fortunato-2017, src-nap-itwiki-grammatica]
- **Archaic literary forms** (`lo, la, li`, `-are` infinitives, `vurria` outside songs) only for deliberate archaic flavour or quoted proverbs.
- **Italianisms:** `ho` for `aggio/tengo`, `devo` for `aggi' 'a`, `andare` for `jì`, `più` for `cchiù`, `molto` for `assaje`, `adesso` for `mo`, `ragazzo` for `guaglione`, `perché` for `pecché`.
- Fake "Neapolitan" from films: don't overuse `uagliò` in every sentence; don't write `ə` or `ë`; never use `w` except in the mode name.
